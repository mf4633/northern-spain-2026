El Eclipse

Tomorrow evening at 8:28, the moon covers the sun over northern Spain — and we'll be standing in its shadow.

La cita · the schedule

One evening, four moments

7:30 PM

We're parked and settled. The show starts soon after, and the good spots will be claimed — this is a hard arrival time, not a suggestion.

~7:35 PM

First bite. The moon starts crossing the sun. Glasses on for every look from here forward. The light slowly turns strange — watch the shadows sharpen.

8:27 – 8:29 PM

TOTALITY. The sun goes out. Glasses OFF — this part is safe and meant for bare eyes. About a minute and a half. Look up, look around, don't fuss with phones.

8:30 PM

The sun returns — glasses back ON the instant the first sliver reappears. The partial phase runs another ~40 minutes as the sun sets.

Times are for the Aguilar de Campoo area and shift by a minute or two with location — Michael has the exact second for wherever we land.

Why we might drive in the afternoon

The coast where we're staying often grows evening cloud off the sea — and this eclipse happens low in the sky at sunset, right where that cloud lives. Michael checks the satellite around 5:30. If the coast looks chancy, we take the pretty drive south over the mountains to the sunny side. Two hours in the car beats missing ninety seconds we came for.

Seguridad · the one rule

Glasses, always — except totality

Never look at the sun without eclipse glasses during the partial phases — not squinting, not "just a second," not through a phone camera. The only exception is the ~90 seconds of totality itself, when the sun is fully covered: then glasses come off, because the corona is faint and completely safe. Michael will call it out loud both ways. Kids' glasses get a grown-up check before every look.

  • Eclipse glasses — one pair each, ISO-certified, no scratches
  • A warm layer — the temperature genuinely drops when the sun goes out
  • Water & snacks — we'll be out from 7:30 to about 9:15
  • Something to sit on — blanket or camp chairs
  • Charged phones — for after; during totality, just watch
La totalidad

What it's actually like

In the last minute, the light goes silver and wrong, like the world through smoked glass. The temperature falls. Birds go quiet.

Then the last bead of sun winks out and there's a black circle in the sky wearing a white crown, with sunset colors on every horizon at once. People gasp. Some cry. It's the best thing the sky does.

And because it's sunset, we get one more gift: the sun comes back just in time to set. Vamos a verlo juntos.
